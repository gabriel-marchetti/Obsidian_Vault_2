# How to read an academic article - Peter Klein.
Link: https://organizationsandmarkets.com/2010/08/31/how-to-read-an-academic-article/

**Motivation**: Reading List in graduate courses become heavy and dense, so becoming an efficient reader is a powerful tool and tends to be a necessary ability to develop - This is, **reading with less effort** and **extract maximum information** - **The Art of the Skim**.

1) Skimming, Scanning and Processing.
	1.a) Read **Abstract**.
	1.b) Read **Introduction**.
	1.c) Read **Conclusion**.
	1.d) Skim middle content - check out **Section Names**, **Section Tables**, **Section Figures** and **Classify** the paper.
		Marks 1) **Methodological**, **Conceptual**, **Theorical**, **Empirical**.
		Marks 2) **Survey**, **Novel Theory Contribution**, **Empirical Application**, **Critique**.
	1.e) Read one time the whole thing at a fast face - don't concern too much about equations, figures and tables.
	1.f) Read the second time with a more carefully approach focusing on sections that seamed important.
2) Once you've got a the main idea of the article/paper - **Critique It**.
	2.a) Make **Questions**... Suggestions (Increase number of questions):
		S1) The argument makes sense? 
		S2) Is it internally consistent?
		S3) Is it supported by argument or evidence?
	2.b) **Compare** the article with other articles you've read before... Make key points:
		S1) Are the arguments consistent, contradictory or orthogonal.
	2.c) Use a searching tool to find other articles talking about it.
	2.d) Check out reference work.
		S1) The author is more inclined into economic articles, but he makes a suggestion to search at the Journal of Economic Literature and see how does it fit in the broader context of the research area - Find similar for computer science.
**OBS - Suggestions from comment section:**
- Skim **Data Sources** - If it is a empirical paper.
	Data sources can reveal strong and weak points of the article.
- Take a look of general formulas to see if it does make sense.

# Advice on reading academic papers - Aaron Massey.
Link: https://userpages.umbc.edu/~akmassey/posts/2012-02-15-advice-on-reading-academic-papers.html

1) Introduction and Conclusion - Identify **problem statement** and **approach taken**.
2) Quick read - determine **Purpose**, **Structure** and **Direction**.
3) **Do not sticky with confusing sections** - mark sections that were confusing and comeback later.
4) After reading identify: **Main points**, **Weakness** and **Strengths**.

# How to read and understand a scientific paper - Jennifer Raff.

**Motivation**: importance of scientific papers - they change the way authorities and procedures are made to influence people's life. So there is a importance in identifying **Scientific Authority**.
- How to know if something is proved? Literature.
- How to know if something is important? Papers.
But papers are **Dense**, have **stilted (unnatural) writing** and **unfamiliar jargon**.
